A Browser-Based Three-Compartment Lung Simulator for Mechanical Ventilation Education: Development and Computational Verification
Philip Bernard
Affiliation: independent · Corresponding: [email redacted] · ORCID: [redacted] · Repo: github.com/DrFillster/medical-vent-twin/lung-reference

Abstract

Objective. To develop a single-file, browser-deployable three-compartment mechanistic lung simulator for mechanical ventilation education, to provide an auditable reference implementation in Python and JavaScript with cross-language verification, and to report computational behaviour with explicit limitation labels.

Methods. We implemented the model twice — once in Python (lung_reference.py v0.1.0, 506 lines, standard library only) and once in JavaScript (lung.js). A 24-test verification suite covers pressure-volume consistency, history-dependent recruitment, gas exchange conservation, mechanical power quadrature, and R/I endpoint arithmetic. cross_check.py enforces absolute/relative agreement at 1e-6 across the four illustrative archetypes and a parameter sensitivity grid. We tested 24 software identities, the four-illustrative-case benchmark, and five additional audit sweeps.

Results. All 24 Python tests pass; all 24 JavaScript tests pass; cross-language agreement at 1e-6 across the benchmark and audit sweeps succeeds. The model produces the following illustrative outputs at the default illustrative presets (PBW 70 kg, AOP 0/2/4/6 cmH₂O across the four cases): Baseline P/F 457 · Injury A P/F 193 · Injury B P/F 111 · Injury C P/F 62 (Pplat 34, exceeds the 30 cmH₂O soft limit and is reported as-is, not adjusted). The signed R/I-style endpoint index was -0.07 / 0.33 / 0.92 / 2.39 respectively. Injury C's R/I exceeds the broad Chen-cohort range (0–2.0); that value is reported visibly rather than clipped or rebadged.

Conclusions. A browser-deployable three-compartment lung simulator can be implemented and cross-checked against an auditable Python reference. The artifact is intended for trainee-driven exploration of quasi-static mechanics, PEEP-history-dependent recruitment, oxygen-content mixing, and decremental P/V curves. It is not a clinical decision tool, has not been bench-validated (no ASL 5000 comparison), has not been face-validated by intensivists, and has not been evaluated against trainee learning outcomes. The bundled Three-Phase Manuscript Workflow "single P/F by Berlin grade" reproduction was dropped; the model is calibrated to physical equations, not clinical-cohort distributions.

Keywords: mechanical ventilation mechanistic lung simulator education PEEP ARDS open-source simulation

1. Introduction

Mechanical ventilation training for critical-care fellows, anesthesiologists, and respiratory therapists relies heavily on expensive and immobile manuscript-grade mannequin simulators. Web-based virtual simulators mitigate the access barrier but historically have either implemented a narrow educational fragment (single-compartment RC, no arterial-oxygenation model) or have made unsupported claims about clinical fidelity. This manuscript describes a quasi-static three-compartment mechanistic lung simulator, with cross-language verification against an auditable Python reference and an explicit enumeration of what the artifact does and does not model.

The model itself draws on three established frameworks: Gattinoni and Pesenti 2005's "baby lung" picture of always-aerated, recruitable, and consolidated tissue[4]; Chen 2020's single-breath PEEP-drop measurement of recruitability[5]; and Severinghaus 1979's saturation equation[9]. Per the technical handoff brief for this revision, the artifact's distinguishing computational property is auditable correctness: every equation lives in two languages, a 24-test verification suite checks internal identities, and a cross-language check enforces agreement at 1e-6.

1.1 Scope and non-claims

The model is a mechanistic simulator, not a digital twin. It is calibrated to physical equations and standard gas-exchange identities. It is not tuned to LUNG SAFE, Bellani 2016, Chen 2020, or any clinical cohort. Each "injury" archetype is an illustrative parameter set, not a patient fit. P/F, R/I, MP and other outputs are model outputs, not measurements of any particular patient.

1.2 Objectives

This work aimed to: (i) implement a quasi-static three-compartment elastic mechanistic lung simulator with one lumped airway resistance, passive constant-flow VCV, and a relay-based PEEP-history recruitment gate; (ii) publish the implementation in two languages (Python source of record, JS browser port) and enforce agreement via a 24-test verification suite; (iii) report the verification results, illustrate the four baseline/injury archetypes that the simulator produces, and enumerate the limitations explicitly.

2. Methods

2.1 Model description

The lung is three compartments with tissue fractions (fN, fR, fC) weighing mechanics; perfusion fractions (qN, qR, qC) (independent of tissue fractions) weighing gas exchange. Normal tissue is always open. Recruitable tissue has open fraction r ∈ [0, 1]. Consolidated tissue has zero mechanical contribution and acts as a permanent shunt. Elastic volume above a common reference is: 

V_i(p) = c_i · k_i · (1 − exp(−p/k_i)),   p = max(P − AOP, 0)
with compartment-specific scales; this is a phenomenological design choice, not a published patient fit (per the handoff brief, §1.7).

2.2 Compliance definitions are consistent

Tidal compliance is Crs_tidal = 1000·Vt/(Pplat−PEEP); tangent compliance is dV/dP = c_i·exp(−p/k_i) at fixed recruitment state. The integration identity holds: ∫PEEPPplat dV = V(Pplat) − V(PEEP). The implementation checks this in software (24-test suite, test_volume_derivative_matches_compliance).

2.3 Recruitment history

Recruitment is implemented as 128 relay thresholds in [closing, opening] order. At each PEEP step, each relay decides based on its own opening and closing thresholds; once opened, a relay stays open until its closing threshold is crossed from above. Each step is instantaneous (no time constant); recruitment is frozen during a breath. The relay thresholds are above AOP; changing AOP shifts their airway-pressure positions. This was demonstrated empirically in the AOP sweep where R/I* peaked at intermediate AOP values and at AOP≥high PEEP the calculation correctly reports invalid.

2.4 Gas exchange (one ventilated alveolar PO₂)

The implementation assumes all ventilated perfusion equilibrates with a common alveolar PO₂; residual perfusion terms are shunt equivalents, not resolved V/Q distributions. O₂ content is conserved across pulmonary capillary mixing: 

CcO2 = 1.34·Hb·S(PAO2) + 0.0031·PAO2
CvO2 = 1.34·Hb·S(PvO2) + 0.0031·PvO2
CaO2 = (1−s)·CcO2 + s·CvO2     # solve for PaO2 such that C(PaO2) = CaO2
Saturation S(P) is the Severinghaus cubic normalized so S(P50)=0.5 exactly; P50 is held fixed (no automatic Bohr/temperature/COHb shift). PAO2 is given by the alveolar gas equation with inspired CO₂ assumed zero. pH is computed from PaCO₂ under fixed bicarbonate (no respiratory compensation). SvO₂ is imposed, not modelled from cardiac output and VO₂. This is the "pure-shunt approximation" as flagged by the handoff brief; Petros 1994[8] provides additional V/Q work that is not adopted here.

2.5 Mechanical power

Mechanical power is the integral of airway pressure relative to atmosphere over the inspired volume, multiplied by RR: 

MP = 0.098 · RR · ∫0Vt P_aw(v) dv
Restricted to passive volume-controlled ventilation with constant inspiratory flow. Pressure-controlled ventilation requires different formulations; assisted ventilation additionally involves patient-generated respiratory muscle work that is not captured by ventilator-delivered mechanical power alone. The displayed mp_linear_estimate is the simplified constant-flow formula retained only as a comparator.

2.6 R/I endpoint analogue (signed)

Following Chen 2020[5] (single-breath PEEP-drop), the calculator steps a state through conditioning → PEEP_high → PEEP_low and computes the signed index 

ΔV       = V(H, rH) − V(L_eff, rL)
expected = C_low · (H − L_eff)
R/I      = (ΔV − expected) / expected
with L_eff = max(PEEP_low, AOP). A conditioning step at 30 cmH₂O is a model-history input, not a clinical recruitment maneuver. Negative values are preserved; never clipped. When AOP ≥ high PEEP the calculator returns {valid: false, reason: "AOP >= high PEEP"} rather than a fabricated value.

2.7 Decremental P/V curve

Compliance at each PEEP step from 20 → 4 cmH₂O after the 30 cmH₂O conditioning; the sampled maximum is reported as such, not as a recommendation. If the maximum lies on a sweep boundary this is flagged (boundary: true). The simulator does not generate a quasi-static inflation/deflation P/V curve and does not estimate a lower inflection point (Pflex).

2.8 Deployment and verification

The artifact is delivered as a sibling directory in the project repo: lung-reference/. The Python source is the contract; lung.js is its faithful port; cross_check.py enforces agreement at 1e-6. Twenty-four unit tests on each side cover input validation, pressure-volume consistency, history dependent recruitment, gas exchange conservation (including zero-shunt and total-shunt limits), and quadrature convergence. The browser UI in index.html exposes the configuration and outputs directly.

3. Results

3.1 Verification

Twenty-four Python tests and twenty-four JavaScript tests pass. cross_check.py succeeds on Baseline, Injury A, Injury B, and Injury C, plus the audit sweeps (power resolution, relay resolution, AOP sweep, Vt sweep, FiO2 sweep at fixed state) at absolute/relative tolerance 1e-6.

3.3 Audit sweeps

Power resolution. Integrated airway work with the exponential elastic law at the Injury C snapshot: 60/120/240/480 intervals yield MP 17.10551408 / 17.10549456 / 17.10548968 / 17.10548846. The 240→480 difference is ≈1.2 × 10⁻⁶ J/min, consistent with the 240-interval default.

Relay resolution. With 64/128/256/512 relays, Injury C R/I* is 2.37 / 2.39 / 2.38 / 2.38 and the sampled compliance maximum stays at PEEP=16. The 128-relay default is consistent with the broader set.

AOP sweep. R/I* peaks at intermediate AOP (1.40 / 2.34 / 2.52 / 2.39 / 1.93 / 1.12 / 0.55 / 0.34 across AOP 0/2/4/6/8/10/12/14). At AOP ≥ 15 cmH₂O the calculator returns valid: false; the failure state is reported rather than coerced.

Vt sensitivity. The endpoint index changes with the low-pressure tidal compliance denominator (R/I* 2.13 / 2.39 / 2.71 / 3.02 / 3.57 across Vt 0.20 / 0.28 / 0.36 / 0.42 / 0.50 L). This is a derivative of the formula, not a feature of any particular patient.

FiO2 sweep at fixed state. At conditioning (30, 14), Injury C PaO₂ rises from 54.8 (FiO₂ 0.21) to 92.9 (FiO₂ 1.00); P/F falls from 261 to 93 because the numerator-PaO₂ gain is sublinear. This sweep uses a single fixed state, so it does not include the recruitment dynamics of a clinician-driven incremental PEEP/FiO₂ protocol.

3.4 Cross-language verification

cross_check.py invokes the same code paths in Python (lung_reference.py) and JavaScript (lung.js via harness.js) and compares matched numerical fields at absolute/relative tolerance 1e-6. All checks pass. Exact-equality comparisons are used for validity flags and relay state.

4. Discussion

4.1 What was verified and what was not

Verified: pressure-volume consistency, history-dependent recruitment and reset, gas exchange conservation, mechanical-power quadrature convergence (against an independent closed form for a single exponential spring with a lumped resistance), R/I endpoint arithmetic, the zero-shunt and total-shunt limits, exact P50 normalization, AOP endpoint handling, and bound checks. The 24-test suite is a software verification, not a physiological validation; a model can be self-consistent and still inaccurate in patient-relevant units.

Not verified: bench comparison (no ASL 5000), expert face validity (no intensivist panel), learner outcomes (no trainee study), gas exchange against measured blood gases in real patients, mechanical power against an independent nonlinear-pressure-curve implementation.

4.2 Misinterpretation risks

  1. P/F by severity is illustrative, not calibrated. The four cases sit in overlapping P/F bands; this is expected because they were not fit to LUNG SAFE. The simulator should not be cited as reproducing the cohort distribution.
  2. The signed R/I-style index is not Chen R/I. The Chen study measures the actual single-breath expired volume when PEEP is stepped 15 → 5 cmH₂O. The simulator derives an analogous volume from its mechanistic volume law. The two are not directly comparable numerically; they are directions-comparable.
  3. Injury C Pplat 34 cmH₂O is a visible model output, not a recommendation. It is reported as-is rather than adjusted. The simulator does not compute a recommended PEEP.
  4. The sampled compliance maximum is not "best PEEP". When the maximum lies on the sweep boundary (boundary: true), the value should not be read as an optimum. The 30 cmH₂O conditioning is a model-history input, not a clinical maneuver recommendation.

4.3 Relation to prior web-based simulators

Review articles[2] identify a number of web-based mechanical-ventilation simulators with various scopes. None, to our knowledge, publishes the model equations and a cross-language verification suite at 1e-6 in a single repository. The handoff brief cites OPENPediatrics and CURE-Soft as exemplars; neither makes the audit infrastructure claimed here. No priority-of-invention claim is made.

4.4 Open-source and licensing

This artifact is published at the project repository (lung-reference/) without a stated open-source license; that status, plus any republication requirements, should be confirmed before any external distribution.

4.5 Limitations

  1. Quasi-static mechanics. Parallel RC transients, expiration, auto-PEEP, and intratidal recruitment are not modelled. Each breath is a settled steady state.
  2. Pure-shunt gas exchange. Ventilated perfusion is collapsed to a common alveolar PO₂; resolved V/Q distributions are absent.
  3. Fixed P50, no Bohr/temperature/COHb shift. The saturation curve is shifted by the user (P50 input), not by blood gas state.
  4. Illustrative parameters. The four cases are not fit to LUNG SAFE, Chen, or any other cohort. Reporting the outputs as "Berlin-grade behaviour" would overstate the model.
  5. No bench validation. The simulator has not been compared against the ASL 5000 or any other mechanical test lung.
  6. No expert face validity. No intensivists or respiratory therapists have rated apparent realism.
  7. No learner-outcome validation. No trainee study has measured educational effect.
  8. No hemodynamic coupling. Intrathoracic-pressure effects on venous return are not modelled.
  9. Browser deployment. Models of patient effort, secretion distribution, secretion clearance, and equipment failure are not in scope.

5. Conclusions

A browser-deployable three-compartment lung simulator can be implemented with cross-language agreement at 1e-6 against an auditable Python reference. The artifact demonstrates the deterministic behaviour of quasi-static mechanics, relay-based PEEP-history recruitment, content-conserving gas exchange, and signed R/I endpoint arithmetic. The model has been computationally verified; it has not been patient-validated. Distinguishing these two conditions is the central safety message of this manuscript.

Next planned validation steps, in order: (i) bench comparison (ASL 5000); (ii) expert face-validity panel; (iii) learner-outcome study. Until those steps are completed, the simulator should be positioned as an educational exploration tool and not as evidence that any particular ventilation strategy is optimal.
