Adult respiratory digital twin research

From physiologic simulation to patient-specific prediction.

A research program using MIMIC data to test whether a mechanistic, HumMod-informed cardiopulmonary digital twin can predict real physiologic responses to changes in mechanical ventilation.

Retrospective validationMechanistic modelingMechanical ventilationCounterfactual simulationMIMIC

Central hypothesis

A mechanistic cardiopulmonary digital twin initialized from routinely collected ICU data can predict the direction and magnitude of physiologic responses to changes in mechanical ventilation better than static associations or simple empirical baselines.

The first objective is not to prove that the simulator is plausible. It is to test whether the model can make patient-specific predictions using only information available before a real clinical intervention.

1 · ObserveIdentify ventilator-transition episodes and the pre-intervention patient state.
2 · InitializeEstimate only the model states and parameters supported by data available at that time.
3 · PredictFreeze the model, apply the observed ventilator change, and simulate forward without post-intervention data.
4 · ValidateCompare predicted trajectories with measured physiology and prespecified baselines.

Specific Aims

The program is structured so that each aim can stand alone as a publishable study while advancing toward patient-specific counterfactual prediction.

Aim 1

Establish identifiability from routine ICU data

Determine whether MIMIC contains enough temporally linked ventilator, gas-exchange, hemodynamic, laboratory, and treatment data to initialize a reduced patient-specific cardiopulmonary twin without using future information.

Key output: a traceable map of directly observed, estimated, weakly identified, and prior-constrained model states.

Aim 2

Predict the response to real ventilator changes

Use naturally occurring changes in PEEP, FiO₂, tidal volume, respiratory rate, minute ventilation, and ventilator mode when adequately documented. Predict subsequent PaCO₂, pH, oxygenation, heart rate, MAP, and available respiratory mechanics.

Primary test: mechanistic twin versus persistence, population-average response, and empirical regression baselines.

Aim 3

Validate coupled cardiopulmonary physiology

Test whether the model reproduces integrated responses to hypoxemia, hypercapnia, acidemia, increased intrathoracic pressure, and changing pulmonary vascular load rather than treating respiratory and cardiovascular outputs as independent predictions.

Aim 4

Derive mechanistic patient phenotypes

Estimate latent physiologic characteristics needed to explain observed responses, including effective compliance, resistance, recruitability, shunt/V-Q abnormality, CO₂ elimination, autonomic responsiveness, and cardiovascular sensitivity to airway pressure.

Aim 5

Evaluate counterfactual ventilator prediction

At the same estimated physiologic starting point, simulate multiple clinically plausible ventilator strategies and test whether the model correctly ranks expected responses. This aim tests treatment-response prediction, not treatment recommendation.

Aim 6

Add high-resolution physiologic signals

In patients with linked waveform data, test whether continuous arterial pressure, ECG/heart rate, respiratory signals, photoplethysmography, and other usable signals improve state estimation, transition detection, and patient-effort characterization.

Aim 7

Determine when mechanism adds value beyond machine learning

Compare simple clinical baselines, conventional statistical models, modern time-series models, the mechanistic digital twin, and hybrid approaches that use learned parameter estimation followed by mechanistic simulation. Evaluate prediction error, calibration, intervention response, physiologic constraint violations, data requirements, and performance outside common training states.

Initial study proposal

The first study should stay narrow: adult invasive ventilation, manually verified transitions, patient state defined before the intervention, and blinded forward prediction.

Population

Mechanically ventilated adults

Construct a cohort of adult ICU episodes with sufficient pre- and post-transition data for ventilator settings, arterial blood gases, oxygenation, heart rate, blood pressure, and relevant treatment context.

Index event

Ventilator transition

A discrete, time-stamped change in one or more ventilator settings that can be distinguished from data-entry artifacts and simultaneous major interventions.

Prediction window

Forward only

Initialize the twin from information available before the index event. Do not use post-transition measurements for calibration of that prediction episode.

Primary outputs

Physiologic trajectory

  • PaCO₂ and pH
  • PaO₂ and/or SpO₂
  • Heart rate and MAP
  • Available respiratory mechanics
  • Direction, magnitude, and timing of change
Comparators

Baselines that are hard to beat unfairly

  • Persistence / no-change forecast
  • Population-average response
  • Simple empirical regression
  • Later: time-series ML and hybrid models

Validation principles

PrincipleImplementation
No future leakageOnly data available before the ventilator transition may initialize the prediction episode.
Traceable parameterizationEach patient-specific state is labeled as observed, estimated, prior-constrained, or not identifiable.
Manual verification firstBegin with a smaller manually reviewed transition cohort before scaling automated extraction.
Prediction, not curve fittingThe model is frozen before the post-intervention outcome window is revealed to the prediction pipeline.
Mechanistic uncertaintyReport prediction intervals or scenario envelopes when parameters are weakly identified.
Clinical contextFlag or exclude transitions confounded by major simultaneous interventions that materially change cardiopulmonary physiology.
Counterfactual restraintAlternative ventilator simulations are research predictions, not bedside treatment recommendations.

Research roadmap

Phase 1
Aims 1–2

Feasibility + prediction

Build the extraction pipeline, manually review transitions, define identifiability, and test forward prediction against simple baselines.

Phase 2
Aim 3

Integrated physiology

Stress-test the coupled respiratory, cardiovascular, gas-exchange, and autonomic response under clinically important states.

Phase 3
Aims 4–7

Phenotypes + counterfactuals

Move from reconstruction toward mechanistic phenotyping, waveform enrichment, hybrid modeling, and counterfactual ranking.

First-paper target: Patient-specific prediction of physiologic response to mechanical ventilation using a mechanistic cardiopulmonary digital twin: retrospective validation in MIMIC.
