A Browser-Based Three-Compartment Mechanistic Lung Simulator for Mechanical Ventilation Education: Development and Internal Physiologic Benchmarking
[Author]
Corresponding author: [email] · ORCID: [orcid] · Repo: github.com/DrFillster/medical-vent-twin

Abstract

Background: Mechanical ventilation training for critical care fellows, anesthesiologists, and respiratory therapists relies heavily on high-fidelity mannequin simulators that are expensive, immobile, and limited in physiological scope. Web-based virtual simulators have existed for two decades but none, to our knowledge, implement a three-compartment lung model calibrated against published Berlin-grade ARDS distributions, integrate the Chen 2020 recruitment-to-inflation (R/I) ratio with a decremental PEEP trial, and reproduce the LUNG SAFE cohort's PaO₂/FiO₂ distributions at standard ventilator settings.

Objective: To develop a single-file, browser-deployable three-compartment mechanistic lung simulator for mechanical ventilation education and to report internal physiologic benchmarking against published cohort distributions as a development milestone, not as clinical validation.

Methods: The simulator implements a three-compartment parallel resistance-compliance (RC) model — Normal (always-open "baby lung"), Recruitable (sigmoid PEEP-gated), and Consolidated (never opens) — inspired by Gattinoni and Pesenti's 2005 framework but implemented here as a parameterized archetype-based simulator (NOT a digital twin: no individual patient calibration, no real-time data synchronization). Oxygen-hemoglobin dissociation follows Severinghaus with Bohr, temperature, 2,3-DPG, and carboxyhemoglobin shifts. Gas exchange uses the classical iso-shunt diagram with a FiO₂⁰·⁶ extrapolation (Petros 1994). The Gattinoni 2016 simplified mechanical-power equation is displayed only during passive volume-controlled ventilation with constant inspiratory flow; the simulator does not report this value for pressure-controlled or assisted ventilation, for which different formulations and additional assumptions (including patient-effort contribution to total energy exposure) are required. Mechanical power is presented as an observational cohort risk marker, not as a clinical decision threshold. PEEP titration uses the decremental trial protocol; the simulator reports the "best-Crs PEEP" (the PEEP at which compliance is maximal across the sweep) and labels it accordingly. The simulator does NOT reproduce the S-shaped pressure-volume curve described by Hickling 1998[22] and the decremental-compliance approach analyzed in Hickling 2001[13], and the model output is therefore not directly comparable to Hickling's Pflex. R/I is computed as a model analogue of Chen 2020's single-breath PEEP-drop method (the model derives an analogous volume change from its mechanistic volume law rather than measuring actual single-breath expired volume); endpoints PEEP_high=15 / PEEP_low=5 with airway-opening-pressure (AOP) correction (P_low,eff = max(PEEP_low, AOP); ΔP_eff = PEEP_high − P_low,eff). The 45-patient Chen cohort median R/I of 0.5 is the study's descriptive median, not a clinically validated threshold. We performed internal benchmarking by sweeping four Berlin-grade patient archetypes (Normal, Mild, Moderate, Severe ARDS) at ARDSNet-style default ventilator settings and comparing the simulator's outputs against published distributions from LUNG SAFE (Bellani 2016) and Chen 2020. This is calibration to literature distributions, not face validity in the strict sense (which requires expert-user judgment of apparent realism).

Results: Across the four Berlin-grade archetypes, the simulator produced P/F ratios consistent with the LUNG SAFE IQR after preset recalibration (Normal 449, not a Berlin grade; Mild 219 [LUNG SAFE 200–300]; Moderate 178 [LUNG SAFE 115–180]; Severe 98 [LUNG SAFE 62–104]; versus Berlin thresholds ≥300/200/100 for non/mild/moderate/severe). The model's R/I analogue produced values within the broad range reported by Chen et al (Normal 0.611, Mild 0.544, Moderate 0.531, Severe 0.390 — affected by the archetype's AOP, Crs, and compartment fractions). Across the fixed archetypes, R/I did NOT rank directly with the nominal recruitability parameter. In a controlled within-Severe-ARDS sensitivity analysis holding all other parameters fixed (shunt 0.50, AOP 6, Crs 18, Vt 280 mL, PEEP 14, FiO₂ 0.80), increasing recruitability from 0.10 to 0.70 produced an expected directional increase in R/I (0.112 → 0.183 → 0.255 → 0.325 → 0.390 → 0.442 → 0.456), peaked at 0.70, and rolled off at 0.80 and 0.90 (0.392, 0.258). The decremental PEEP-compliance curve peaked at the top of the sweep in all archetypes (PEEP 20); the simulator reports the best-Crs PEEP but does not generate a quasi-static P/V curve and does not report a Hickling Pflex. All numbers in this manuscript come from the simulator at the commit referenced in Appendix 2 and were regenerated programmatically rather than hand-reconciled.

Conclusions: A three-compartment mechanistic lung simulator can be implemented as a single-file browser artifact whose outputs fall within published physiological cohort ranges when calibrated to literature distributions. The artifact is intended for trainee-driven exploration of ventilator manipulation and gas-exchange trade-offs; it is not a clinical decision tool and has not been validated against bench simulators (e.g. ASL 5000), patient monitors, or learner outcomes. The next planned validation step is ASL 5000 bench testing rather than further preset tuning; clinician expert face-validity and learner-outcome studies are the planned subsequent steps before any claim of medical-education efficacy. This manuscript is a development report and an internal benchmarking milestone — NOT a validation study, NOT a clinical decision tool, and NOT yet peer reviewed.

1. Introduction

Mechanical ventilation is a high-stakes, hands-on competency that is difficult to teach without access to intubated patients or high-fidelity mannequins. Training programs typically rely on a combination of didactic instruction, mannequin-based simulation, and supervised clinical experience. Mannequin-based simulation is effective[1,2] but expensive (capital costs of $50,000–$200,000 per station), immobile, and limited in the physiological scenarios that can be presented.

Web-based virtual simulators — sometimes called "mechanical ventilation virtual simulators" (MVVS) — have existed for over two decades. Examples include the Hamilton G5 trainer, xlung.net[3], VentSim, and OPENPediatrics[15]. Most existing MVVS lack physiological validation studies; a 2024 non-inferiority trial[2] compared a free web-based lung simulator (VentSim) against the ASL 5000 bench simulator for trainee knowledge gain and found comparable educational outcomes, but did not validate the underlying physiological model against published cohort distributions.

Since 2016 the field has advanced in three ways that no single existing browser simulator fully integrates. First, Gattinoni and Pesenti's 2005 "baby lung" framework[4], refined by Cressoni and colleagues using quantitative CT scans, distinguishes three functionally distinct lung compartments: always-aerated tissue, derecruited-but-recruitable tissue, and consolidated tissue that cannot be opened regardless of distending pressure. Second, the recruitment-to-inflation (R/I) ratio, described by Chen and colleagues in 2020[5] in a 45-patient ARDS cohort (median R/I 0.5, range 0–2.0 — a descriptive study median, not a validated clinical threshold), provides a bedside method for quantifying recruitability. Third, the LUNG SAFE observational cohort (Bellani 2016[6], 2,377 ARDS patients across 50 countries) published the first multinational distribution of ventilation parameters and outcomes across Berlin ARDS severity grades, providing a reference distribution against which any simulator's outputs can be compared.

This paper describes a browser-deployable three-compartment mechanistic lung simulator for the mechanically ventilated adult lung that integrates these three advances in a single HTML file, and reports internal physiologic benchmarking of the simulator's outputs against the LUNG SAFE and Chen 2020 reference distributions. "Internal physiologic benchmarking" here means parameter calibration to literature distributions and parameter-sweep verification — it does not constitute face validity in the strict sense (which requires expert-user judgment of apparent realism), external validity (which requires an independent dataset), or bench validity (which requires comparison against an ASL 5000 or equivalent).

1.1 Objectives

Specifically, this work aimed to: (i) implement a three-compartment parallel RC lung model inspired by Gattinoni-Pesenti 2005 with a sigmoid PEEP-gated recruitable compartment and a consolidated compartment that gives a permanent-shunt floor; (ii) integrate Chen 2020's single-breath R/I method with a decremental PEEP trial, including airway-opening-pressure correction; (iii) perform internal physiologic benchmarking of the simulator's PaO₂/FiO₂ (P/F) and R/I outputs against the LUNG SAFE and Chen 2020 reference distributions across four Berlin-grade archetypes; (iv) deploy the result as a single-file browser artifact with no installation step.

2. Methods

2.1 Model architecture

The lung is modeled as three parallel compartments whose fractions sum to one:

  • Normal — always-aerated "baby lung" tissue with normal compliance, low resistance, and low shunt. Fraction fNormal scales inversely with severity, following the CT-derived observation that the baby lung shrinks as ARDS becomes more severe[4].
  • Recruitable — derecruited tissue that opens sigmoidally with PEEP, parameterized by a recruitability score (0–1) and an opening pressure. Contributes residual V/Q mismatch even when fully open, because recruited ARDS tissue has worse gas exchange than normal lung[7].
  • Consolidated — non-recruitable tissue that contributes zero compliance and permanent shunt, irrespective of PEEP. Fraction fConsolidated scales with severity and inversely with recruitability, consistent with the observation that low recruiters have proportionally more non-openable tissue[4,7].

The compartmental compliance sums directly (parallel compliances add, not reciprocals), matching the clinical observation that measured Crs in ARDS often appears "preserved" because the baby lung dominates the parallel sum. The consolidated compartment's zero compliance gives the overall respiratory system a hard compliance floor: the system Crs equals the functional (Normal + Recruitable) Crs weighted by their fractions, with no contribution from consolidated tissue.

2.2 Effective shunt

The effective shunt for gas exchange is computed as the perfusion-weighted fraction of non-gas-exchanging perfusion across all three compartments:

eff_shunt = fNormal · shuntNormal
            + fRecruitable · openness · shuntRecruitedResidual
            + fRecruitable · (1 − openness) · 1.0
            + fConsolidated · 1.0

where shuntNormal = max(0.02, true_shunt · 0.20) and shuntRecruitedResidual = max(0.05, true_shunt · 0.40). The two residual terms ensure that eff_shunt does not collapse toward baby-lung baseline at full recruitment — a defect in earlier two-compartment formulations that caused severe ARDS to read as PaO₂/FiO₂ > 300 ("Normal") after recruitment. Calibration target: eff_shunt ≈ 0.65 · true_shunt at moderate PEEP, matching the LUNG SAFE P/F distributions per Berlin grade[7].

2.3 Oxygen-hemoglobin dissociation

PaO₂ is computed from eff_shunt using the classical iso-shunt diagram (Shapiro, West, Feiner) with a FiO₂⁰·⁶ extrapolation per Petros 1994[8]. SpO₂ is computed from PaO₂ using a normalized Severinghaus curve[9] indexed by PaO₂/P50, with P50 shifted for temperature (1.018ΔT), Bohr effect (pH), chronic 2,3-DPG, and carboxyhemoglobin (COHb) artifact on pulse oximetry[10]. The normalized-curve approach is shift-invariant and immune to sigmoid tuning errors.

2.4 Mechanical power

The Gattinoni simplified equation is displayed only during passive volume-controlled ventilation with constant inspiratory flow: MP = 0.098 · RR · Vt · (Ppeak − 0.5 · ΔP)[11]. The simulator does NOT report this value for pressure-controlled or assisted ventilation, for which different formulations and additional assumptions are required (Costa 2023). Patient effort in assisted modes contributes to total energy exposure and cannot be derived from ventilator pressure–volume waveforms alone, so any MP estimate under assisted ventilation would require a separate model of patient respiratory muscle work that is out of scope for this artifact. The MP/ΔP ratio (MP_per_dp) is reported as a Costa 2023 resistive-work index. MP is presented as an OBSERVATIONAL COHORT RISK MARKER, not as a validated individual-patient safety limit: MP > 17 J/min is the Serpa Neto 2018 cohort inflection[12], not a clinical decision threshold. The simulator does NOT use MP in isolation to drive recommendations about sedation depth, neuromuscular blockade, or extracorporeal support. Alarms fire at ΔP > 15 cmH₂O and Pplat > 30 cmH₂O (ARDSNet limits); the MP > 17 line is rendered as a flag, not an alarm.

2.5 Decremental PEEP trial and R/I ratio

The simulator reports compliance at each PEEP level during a decremental sweep. It does not generate a conventional quasi-static inflation/deflation pressure-volume curve and therefore does not estimate a lower inflection point (Pflex). The trial steps PEEP from 20 → 4 cmH₂O in 2-cmH₂O decrements following a recruitment maneuver; for each PEEP step the simulator computes Crs at a single Vt breath and records the resulting decremental PEEP-compliance curve. The simulator computes a model analogue of Chen 2020's single-breath PEEP-drop method[5]: the Chen study measures the actual single-breath expired volume when PEEP is stepped 15 → 5 cmH₂O in a sedated/paralyzed patient, whereas the simulator derives an analogous volume change from its mechanistic volume law. We describe the simulator output as a model analogue of Chen R/I (NOT a literal bedside measurement). Endpoints: PEEP_high = 15, PEEP_low = 5 (canonical Chen endpoints). In quasi-static form:

ΔEELVmodel = EELV(PEEPhigh) − EELV(Plow,eff)
  where EELV(p) = max(0, p − AOP) · Crs(max(0, p − AOP))
Plow,eff = max(PEEPlow, AOP) = max(5, AOP)
ΔPeff = PEEPhigh − Plow,eff
Vexpected = Crs(Plow,eff) × ΔPeff
Vrecruited = max(0, ΔEELVmodel − Vexpected)
Crecruited = Vrecruited / ΔPeff
R/I = Crecruited / Crs(Plow,eff)

Caveats. Crs in the code is the three-compartment pressure-dependent compliance (Crs(p) = crsNormal(p) + crsRecruitable(p)·fRecruitable·openness(p)), so the closed form V(p) = (p−AOP)·Crs(p−AOP) is itself an approximation that treats Crs as locally constant between the high and low PEEP steps. We do not simplify ΔEELVmodel further than this; we compute EELV(15) and EELV(Plow,eff) directly from the simulator volume law. This avoids the false-equation Vexpired = (15−AOP)×Crs(15−AOP) that an earlier draft displayed.

When AOP ≤ PEEPlow (the Normal archetype), Plow,eff = PEEPlow = 5 and ΔPeff = 10, recovering Chen's original endpoints. When AOP > PEEPlow (the Severe archetype with AOP = 6), Plow,eff = 6, ΔPeff = 9, and Crs is measured at the effective low pressure (6 cmH₂O) rather than at the nominal PEEPlow (5 cmH₂O). The four archetype AOP values (0, 2, 4, 6 cmH₂O) were chosen as an archetype design choice to demonstrate airway-closure handling across the severity range; they are not derived from a specific cohort measurement of airway opening pressure distributions in ARDS. Airway closure is real and clinically important, but its prevalence and regional behavior are heterogeneous, so the preset gradient should be read as illustrative of the model's behavior rather than as a population estimate.

R/I is presented as a CONTINUOUS variable. Chen 2020 enrolled 45 ARDS patients and reported a cohort median R/I of 0.5 with range 0–2.0; the 0.5 value is descriptive of that cohort distribution and is NOT a clinically validated universal decision threshold. We do not assign recruiter tier labels.

2.6 ARDSNet enforcement

When the global ARDSNet mode toggle is enabled, the simulator snaps Vt to vtPerKg × PBW in volume control (default 6 mL/kg PBW per Brower 2000[19]), recomputes driving pressure, mechanical power, and Pplat. The two preset ARDSNet ladders (NIH ARDSMA lower-PEEP and ARDSMB higher-PEEP) drive the optimizer, which finds the PEEP/FiO₂ combination on the chosen ladder that minimizes mechanical power while keeping SpO₂ above the patient-specific target; hard penalties dominate the cost function so safety constraints cannot be traded for lower MP. PBW is the Devine-formula predicted body weight[14], not actual weight.

The four clinical archetypes in this manuscript set ardsnetOn: false in PRESETS so the tabular Vt is what the simulator actually uses. Without this disable, the simulation would silently snap Mild's 420, Moderate's 360, and Severe's 280 up to 6 mL/kg PBW (420 mL). With ardsnetOn: false, the verbatim preset values are retained at runtime. Mild (420 mL = 6.00 mL/kg) is the ARDSNet-low target; Moderate (360 mL = 5.14 mL/kg) and Severe (280 mL = 4.00 mL/kg) fall within the Brower 2000 allowed range of 4–6 mL/kg, with Severe's 4 mL/kg a permitted further reduction when 6 mL/kg would produce Pplat > 30 cmH₂O. The user-facing ARDSNet mode toggle still works for the live simulator and is the recommended default for ad-hoc exploration.

2.7 Deployment

The simulator is implemented as a single HTML file (~70 KB, ~1,475 lines) containing the model, UI, optimizer, and decremental-PEEP-trial logic. No build step, no dependencies, no network calls. The file is served via a local HTTP server bound to 127.0.0.1 and exposed publicly through an existing Cloudflare tunnel at vent.defying-logic.com. The repo is publicly archived at github.com/DrFillster/medical-vent-twin. Edit-deploy iteration time is under one second; users can save the page locally and use it offline.

2.8 Internal physiologic benchmarking

We performed internal physiologic benchmarking by sweeping four patient archetypes — Normal, Mild ARDS, Moderate ARDS, Severe ARDS — at preset ventilator settings (Vt, PEEP, FiO₂, RR as per Table 3) and comparing the simulator's outputs against the LUNG SAFE (Bellani 2016[6]) reference distributions for P/F and the Chen 2020 R/I cohort[5]. Specifically, we tested: (i) that P/F ratios fall within the LUNG SAFE IQR per Berlin grade after preset shunt recalibration; (ii) that the model's R/I analogue reflects recruitability directionally in a controlled within-archetype sweep, holding all other Severe-ARDS parameters fixed and varying recruitability 0.10–0.90; (iii) that the decremental PEEP trial produces a decremental PEEP-compliance curve on which the best-Crs PEEP (related to Hickling 2001 but NOT a sigmoid P/V curve and NOT Hickling's Pflex) can be reported and labeled correctly.

Preset parameters (Crs, shunt, recruitability, AOP, Vt, RR, monitor outputs) for each archetype are reproduced in Table 3. Benchmarking was performed by running the simulator against the four archetype parameter sets and tabulating the outputs; no clinical data were used and no human subjects were involved. This is calibration to literature distributions, not external validation in the sense of independent dataset comparison or expert face-validity judgment.

3. Results

3.1 Berlin-grade P/F reproduction

Across the four archetypes at default ventilator settings, the simulator produced P/F ratios consistent with the LUNG SAFE IQR per Berlin grade (Table 1) after preset shunt recalibration (Normal shunt 0.05 → Mild 0.16 → Moderate 0.38 → Severe 0.50). The Normal archetype produced P/F 449 (not a Berlin grade; LUNG SAFE non-ARDS median ~400); Mild ARDS produced P/F 219 (LUNG SAFE mild median ~250, IQR 200–300); Moderate ARDS produced P/F 178 (LUNG SAFE moderate median ~150, IQR 115–180); Severe ARDS produced P/F 98 (LUNG SAFE severe median ~80, IQR 62–104). The P/F ordering across grades was preserved (Normal > Mild > Moderate > Severe), matching the Berlin definition's monotonicity requirement, and all three ARDS-grade outputs land within or just above the LUNG SAFE IQR for their respective grades.

3.2 R/I ratio (model analogue, not literal bedside measurement)

The simulator does not literally reproduce Chen 2020's bedside measurement. The Chen method measures the actual single-breath expired volume when PEEP is stepped from 15 → 5 cmH₂O; the simulator computes a model analogue of that measurement from its mechanistic volume law (ΔEELVmodel = EELVhigh − EELVlow,effective). Reporting the model output as "Chen R/I" would overstate the simulator's fidelity; we therefore describe the simulator output as a "model analogue of the Chen 2020 single-breath R/I method." All R/I values in this manuscript refer to that model analogue.

Across the fixed archetypes, R/I values ranged from 0.39 to 0.61 (Table 2). R/I reflected the combined effects of recruitability, respiratory-system compliance, compartment fractions, airway-opening pressure, and the non-linear recruitment function; therefore archetype R/I did not rank directly with the single nominal recruitability parameter (Normal 0.10 → 0.611; Mild 0.40 → 0.544; Moderate 0.60 → 0.531; Severe 0.50 → 0.390). The Severe archetype's lower R/I compared with the less-severe archetypes arose from its higher AOP forcing a smaller ΔPeff (9 cmH₂O instead of 10) and from its higher shunt driving the lung into the lower part of the sigmoid recruitment curve.

Within-archetype sensitivity. Holding the remaining Severe-ARDS parameters fixed (shunt 0.50, AOP 6, Crs 18, Vt 280, PEEP 14) and varying recruitability from 0.10 to 0.90 produced the expected directional increase in R/I for recruit ≤ 0.70 (R/I 0.112 at 0.10, 0.183 at 0.20, 0.255 at 0.30, 0.325 at 0.40, 0.390 at 0.50, 0.442 at 0.60, 0.456 at 0.70), peaked at 0.70 (R/I 0.456), and rolled off at higher recruitability values (0.392 at 0.80, 0.258 at 0.90) due to the sigmoid recruitment gate plateauing and the Vexpected term growing faster than Vexpired. We do not claim strict monotonicity across the full 0.10–0.90 range; the controlled sweep confirms the expected direction with a peak in the moderate-to-high recruitability region, consistent with Chen 2020's observation that the original cohort R/I distribution spans 0–2.0 (median 0.5).

3.3 Decremental PEEP trial curve shape

The decremental PEEP-compliance curve (compliance at each PEEP step from 20 → 4 cmH₂O in 2-cmH₂O decrements) is NOT a quasi-static inflation/deflation pressure-volume curve and the simulator does NOT report a lower inflection point (Pflex). In every archetype, the simulator's compliance is maximal at the top of the sweep (PEEP 20), so the 'best-Crs PEEP' was 20 across all four. This reflects the simulator's lack of an overdistension mechanism — in models that do not penalize high PEEP, Crs rises monotonically with PEEP because the sigmoid recruitment gate keeps opening. The simulator therefore cannot reproduce the S-shaped P/V curve described in Hickling 1998[22] and does not report a lower inflection point (Pflex). The simulator output should be read as a decremental PEEP-compliance curve, not as a static P/V curve. This is a documented limitation, not a calibration failure. The simulator remains useful for teaching the decremental PEEP trial concept, the directional effect of PEEP on Crs in moderate-to-severe ARDS, and the relationship between recruitability and the magnitude of Crs change across PEEP steps.

3.4 Monitor values at default settings

For the Severe ARDS archetype at default settings (Vt 280 mL = 4.0 mL/kg PBW for PBW 70 kg, PEEP 14, FiO₂ 0.80, RR 26, mode VC), the simulator produced ΔP 12.0 cmH₂O, Pplat 26.0 cmH₂O, PaO₂ 78 mmHg, PaCO₂ ~40 mmHg, pH ~7.40, and mechanical power 18.6 J/min. ARDSNet LTV protocol target met: Pplat ≤ 30 cmH₂O ✓. The Vt of 280 mL (= 4 mL/kg PBW) is a deliberate below-target choice (ARDSNet allowed range 4–6 mL/kg PBW per Brower 2000[19]) reflecting the clinical convention of reducing Vt when 6 mL/kg would produce Pplat > 30 cmH₂O. The MP = 18.6 J/min is above the Serpa Neto 2018 cohort inflection of 17 J/min and is reported as an observational risk marker, not as a basis for clinical decisions about sedation depth, neuromuscular blockade, or extracorporeal support.

4. Discussion

4.1 Principal findings

This work demonstrates that a three-compartment mechanistic lung simulator of the mechanically ventilated lung — implemented in a single ~70 KB HTML file — can reproduce the published Berlin-grade P/F distributions from LUNG SAFE and the R/I distribution from Chen 2020 across four severity archetypes after preset shunt calibration. The simulator is freely accessible, requires no installation, and supports trainee-driven exploration of ventilator manipulation, lung recruitment, and gas exchange trade-offs. To our knowledge, no previously published web-based mechanical ventilation simulator integrates all three of the Gattinoni-Pesenti three-compartment framework, the Chen 2020 single-breath R/I method, and the LUNG SAFE Berlin-grade distributions in a single browser artifact with internal physiologic benchmarking. The model is, however, a parameterized archetype-based simulator and not a digital twin in the sense used in industrial cybernetics (which requires individual calibration from a real patient and continuous synchronization with patient monitoring).

4.2 Comparison with prior work

The eight most-cited web-based MVVS include the Hamilton G5 trainer, xlung.net[3], VentSim, and OPENPediatrics[15]; all but the last two are proprietary commercial simulators with limited published validation data. The two open-source web simulators, xlung and OPENPediatrics, focus on ventilator knob training and pediatric scenarios respectively; neither implements the Gattinoni-Pesenti three-compartment framework or the Chen 2020 R/I methodology. Subsequent validation work (Safadi 2024 Respiratory Care[2]) compared VentSim against the ASL 5000 bench simulator for trainee knowledge gain but did not validate the underlying physiological model against published cohort distributions. Our work addresses the validation gap that earlier simulator studies identified.

Among computational physiology models of mechanical ventilation, the most mature is the Aalborg BEACON/CURE-Soft model (Rees, Karbing, Allerød et al.), which has been clinically validated in randomized trials including the recent DEVENT trial (Patel 2024 Frontiers in Medicine[16]). BEACON is a more elaborate physiological model with explicit gas-exchange equations and V/Q mismatch distributions, but it requires server-side computation and is not a browser artifact. The Joy 2025 Critical Care Medicine[17] VILI simulation study used a high-fidelity cardiopulmonary simulator that requires MATLAB; it is a research tool, not an educational web artifact. Our work occupies a different niche: a lightweight browser-based simulator that captures the key pedagogical phenomena (Berlin-grade gas exchange, recruitable-vs-non-recruitable tissue, Chen 2020 R/I) at the cost of physiological granularity and at the gain of zero-install deployment.

4.3 Limitations

This manuscript and the underlying model have the following specific limitations. Each is named explicitly to prevent overinterpretation.

  1. Internal benchmarking is calibration, not external validation. The simulator's preset shunt values were chosen to reproduce the LUNG SAFE IQR per Berlin grade at the preset ventilator settings; outputs that match the calibration target are therefore partly circular. An independent dataset (a separate ARDS cohort not used to choose shunt values) would strengthen the claim; no such external validation has been performed.
  2. R/I is a model analogue, not a literal bedside measurement. The simulator computes ΔEELV from its mechanistic volume law, not from a measured single-breath expired volume. The closed-form approximation Vexpired = (15−AOP) · Crs(15−AOP) used in an earlier draft was an approximation that treats Crs as locally constant between the high and low PEEP steps; the current implementation computes EELV(15) and EELV(Plow,eff) directly. Comparisons to Chen 2020 are directional, not numerical.
  3. Four-archetype R/I does not rank directly with the recruitability parameter. Across the fixed archetypes, R/I values ranged 0.39–0.61 (Normal 0.611, Mild 0.544, Moderate 0.531, Severe 0.390) and reflected the combined effects of recruitability, Crs, compartment fractions, AOP, and the non-linear recruitment function; therefore archetype R/I does not rank directly with the single user-facing recruitability input parameter. The within-Severe sensitivity sweep produced monotonic increase from recruit=0.10 (R/I 0.112) to recruit=0.70 (R/I 0.456), peaked at 0.70, and rolled off to 0.258 at 0.90; the simulator should not be cited as a tool for measuring recruitability from R/I alone.
  4. No overdistension mechanism. The model lacks an overdistension penalty on Crs at high PEEP, so the decremental PEEP-compliance curve rises monotonically with PEEP and the best-Crs PEEP sits at the top of the sweep across all archetypes.
  5. No conventional quasi-static P/V curve and no Pflex. The simulator does NOT generate a sigmoid pressure-volume curve. It does not report a Pflex. The "decremental PEEP-compliance curve" output is related conceptually to the decremental-compliance analysis in Hickling 2001[13] but is not the static P/V curve discussed in Hickling 1998[22].
  6. MP formula restricted to passive constant-flow VCV. The simplified Gattinoni equation implemented here is restricted to passive volume-controlled ventilation with constant inspiratory flow. Pressure-controlled ventilation requires different formulations, and assisted ventilation additionally involves patient-generated respiratory muscle work that is not captured by ventilator-delivered mechanical power alone.
  7. Simplified gas exchange. The iso-shunt diagram with FiO₂⁰·⁶ extrapolation is a simplified V/Q model; a more elaborate two-compartment V/Q mismatch formulation (Karbing 2007) would improve physiological fidelity but is out of scope for the current artifact.
  8. Illustrative, not epidemiologically calibrated, AOP presets. The four archetype AOP values (0, 2, 4, 6 cmH₂O) are an archetype design choice to demonstrate airway-closure handling across severity, not derived from a specific cohort measurement of airway-opening pressure distributions in ARDS.
  9. No hemodynamic coupling. The simulator does not model intrathoracic-pressure → venous-return coupling and therefore cannot show the hemodynamic trade-offs of high-PEEP / recruitment-maneuver strategies.
  10. No ASL 5000 bench validation. The simulator has not been compared against a mechanical test lung.
  11. No clinician expert face-validity panel. No intensivists or respiratory therapists have formally rated the simulator's apparent realism.
  12. No learner-outcome validation. The simulator has not been evaluated in a trainee population against mannequin-based training.
  13. No real-patient calibration. All preset parameters are literature-derived illustrative values, not fitted from individual patients.

ART trial interpretation. ART (Cavalcanti et al. 2017 JAMA[21]) demonstrated harm from the tested bundled strategy of aggressive recruitment maneuvers plus compliance-guided PEEP titration compared with a conventional low-PEEP strategy. ART does not isolate the independent effect of compliance-guided PEEP selection. We do not interpret ART as evidence that the "best-Crs PEEP" concept itself is harmful; the trial cannot identify which component of the recruitment-maneuver + compliance-titrated PEEP bundle caused the harm. We therefore explicitly do NOT recommend "best-Crs PEEP" as a clinical decision rule. The simulator can teach decremental PEEP physiology, but the educational framing must make clear that physiologic optimization of a single variable does not automatically improve clinical outcomes and that compliance-guided PEEP titration has not been validated as a stand-alone strategy.

ARDSNet protocol framing. The original ARDSNet LTV trial (Brower 2000[19]) established Pplat ≤ 30 cmH₂O as the safety limit; Vt reduction to as low as 4 mL/kg PBW was an allowed adjustment when 6 mL/kg produced Pplat > 30. Driving pressure around 14–15 cmH₂O comes from later observational and secondary analyses (Amato 2015[20]) and is not part of the original ARDSNet protocol. The simulator distinguishes between the ARDSNet-protocol Pplat ≤ 30 target and the observational ΔP risk marker (displayed separately).

4.4 Implications for medical education

The simulator is most useful as a complement to, not a replacement for, mannequin-based training. Its strengths — unlimited scenario variety, offline accessibility, instantaneous physiological feedback, and free distribution — make it suitable for asynchronous learning, group problem-based learning sessions, and self-directed exploration of the ARDSNet lower-PEEP and higher-PEEP ladders, decremental PEEP titration, and R/I-driven PEEP selection. Its weaknesses — lack of tactile feedback, lack of hemodynamic coupling, lack of team-based simulation — make it unsuitable for procedural training or crisis resource management. Future work should evaluate learner outcomes (knowledge gain, retention, transfer to mannequin-based scenarios) against mannequin-only and didactic-only controls, following the methodology of Spadaro 2017[1] and Safadi 2024[2].

4.5 Future work

Planned extensions include: (i) integration of the Karbing two-compartment V/Q mismatch model for more accurate low-FiO₂ gas exchange; (ii) ASL 5000 bench validation following the Cour 2022[18] protocol; (iii) clinician expert face-validity panel (≥5 board-certified intensivists rating apparent realism on a 5-point Likert scale across the four archetypes); (iv) a prospective single-center trainee study (N ≥ 40 critical care fellows) comparing pre/post knowledge scores against mannequin-based training per Spadaro 2017[1] and Safadi 2024[2] methodology; (v) extension to pediatric and neonatal ventilation per the OPENPediatrics framework. Note that preset retuning (the prior first planned extension) has been completed in the current revision; all three ARDS-grade P/F outputs now land within the LUNG SAFE IQR.

5. Conclusions

A three-compartment mechanistic lung simulator of the mechanically ventilated lung can be implemented as a single-file browser artifact and benchmarked against published physiological distributions from LUNG SAFE and Chen 2020. The simulator reproduces the Berlin-grade P/F ordering across four severity archetypes (Normal 449, Mild 219, Moderate 178, Severe 98 — all ARDS-grade outputs within the LUNG SAFE IQR after preset recalibration). Across the fixed archetypes, the model's R/I analogue did not rank directly with the single nominal recruitability parameter; in a controlled within-Severe-ARDS sweep holding all other parameters fixed, R/I increased monotonically from 0.112 at recruit=0.10 to 0.456 at recruit=0.70, then rolled off to 0.258 at recruit=0.90 — a partial-sweep directional increase consistent with Chen 2020's published bedside measurement but not full-range monotonic. The freely accessible deployment (vent.defying-logic.com) supports trainee-driven exploration of ventilator manipulation, recruitment, and gas-exchange trade-offs.

This is a development report and an internal physiologic benchmarking milestone — NOT a validation study, NOT a clinical decision tool, and NOT yet peer reviewed. The next planned validation steps are (in order): ASL 5000 bench testing; a clinician expert face-validity panel; a learner-outcome study. Until those steps are completed, the simulator should be positioned as an educational exploration tool and not as evidence that any particular ventilation strategy is optimal.
